# A tool's bar

TRA-98: "the bar of Chat, Docs, Announcements, Activity and Settings like the
tasks'."

## What it was

Tasks had a bar: its name on the left with a round plus beside it, and how
the list is drawn on the right. The other five each had their own idea —
Chat and Announcements had no name at all, Docs had four pills and no name,
Activity carried its name in the body a row lower, Settings started with a
section heading. Switching tabs moved the name and changed what the bar was
for.

## What it is

One shape on every tab, in the bar slot (`Toolbar`):

- **Left:** the tab's name (`PageTitle`), and the one thing the tab makes as
  a round plus beside it — a channel, a document, an announcement. A tab
  that makes nothing (Activity, Settings) has the name alone. A reader who
  may not write still sees the name.
- **Right:** what else the tab can do, or how it is looked at — the rooms in
  Chat, Upload · New folder · Add link in Docs, Ask every week in
  Announcements.

The name and the plus are there before the tab's data is: Activity and
Settings draw their bar while the feed and the five sections load, so the
bar does not fill in a beat late.

## On a phone

The name is the tab menu's, so `PageTitle` keeps only the plus.
The right side shows marks without words — upload, folder, link, the
recurring mark for a check-in — because three pills and the tab menu are
wider than the bar. Chat's plus goes to the end of the row, where the panel
it opens has room; the rooms shrink and scroll sideways rather than spilling
over the tab menu, which is what they did.

A round plus opens into the same field the pill did, narrower on a phone.

## The bar over its column

The bar is as wide as the work under it again: Tasks the window's width,
and Chat, Docs, Announcements, Activity and Settings at the chat's measure —
eight hundred and the gutter either side — with their columns of work held
to the same. For a while the bar ran the window's width on every tool, and
over a centred column it put the name far to the left of what it named. A
room keeps room for a thread beside it in its column; its bar stays at the
room's measure.
